# Virality: sound and hosted tools

Research checked 2026-11-21. Scope: authorized GTA 6 streamer clips for TikTok/Reels/Shorts and a view-paid Content Rewards campaign (task context; unsourced). Prices are USD, monthly billing unless stated; vendor claims are not independent performance tests.

## A1. Do added sound effects measurably help?

- **No direct causal evidence found** for a boom, zoom, riser or meme stinger increasing views/retention on organic gaming/streamer clips across these three platforms (search finding; unsourced). Do not attach a percentage uplift to this edit.
- Closest platform evidence: TikTok/Kantar reports 73% of respondents would stop and look at TikTok ads with audio, and 88% considered sound essential. These are survey/ad findings about sound generally, not an experiment adding SFX to an already audible streamer clip; speech/game audio already supplies sound. [TikTok/Kantar](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok)
- TikTok recommends background sounds/effects for gaming ads, but supplies no isolated SFX uplift in this guidance. This supports a creative hypothesis, not a promised organic-view benefit. [Gaming creative guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok-hyper-casual-game-creative-tips/pc/en)
- **Recommendation (unsourced proposal):** default to original reaction/game audio; optionally use one restrained accent at the payoff or a riser ending there. Avoid covering the punchline. Test SFX/no-SFX across randomized comparable clips, stratified by platform; compare completion, average watch time, shares and eligible paid views at the same age. Do not publish duplicate variants merely to run the test.

## A2. Background music: rights, availability and campaign economics

- **TikTok:** promotional content should use the Commercial Music Library (CML); TikTok's licenses for music outside it do not cover commercial use. Outside-CML/original audio requires confirmation that no protected music is present or all necessary licenses are held. This applies to promotional content, not just accounts labelled Business. [Commercial music help](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok)
- Business accounts see Commercial Sounds when adding audio; CML selection must match the campaign region and allowed placements. A TikTok clearance is not evidence of permission to bake the same track into Reels/Shorts exports: obtain separate cross-platform rights before doing so (inference). [CML usage](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-the-commercial-music-library?lang=en-GB), [CML scope](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/commercial-music-library?lang=en)
- **Instagram:** the licensed music library is intended for personal/noncommercial use, with restrictions by business account, post type and region; Meta points commercial users toward Sound Collection. The primary help URL redirected to login during this research, so current wording was **not directly reverified**; a secondary source reproduces it. Do not assume switching to Creator clears campaign music. [Primary help](https://help.instagram.com/402084904469945/), [secondary reproduction](https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-business-account-music)
- **YouTube/Content ID:** a matching upload can be blocked, monetized by the rights holder or tracked, including country-specific treatment; a Content ID claim is distinct from a copyright takedown/strike. A playable claimed clip therefore is not proof of clearance. [YouTube claims](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276)
- **Mute/reach:** TikTok can mute every video using a removed sound; Meta documents matching/blocking tools, and Pixabay warns that other platforms may mute/block even licensed tracks. No evidence found for a universal percentage reach penalty merely from adding background music (unsourced search finding); lost availability and a muted reaction are the concrete risks. [TikTok removals](https://support.tiktok.com/en/log-in-troubleshoot/troubleshooting/a-sound-i-added-to-tiktok-was-removed), [Meta rights tools](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/facebook-ip-protection.pdf), [Pixabay claims FAQ](https://pixabay.com/service/faq/)
- **Existing stream music matters too:** streamer permission does not automatically clear third-party songs/game-radio recordings for a promotional re-edit (inference). Audit the source mix as well as added tracks; prefer a clean recording/audio track where available. TikTok requires rights for protected audio and Freesound likewise warns that an uploader's licence label may be invalid. [TikTok rights](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok), [Freesound provenance warning](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- **Content Rewards:** Whop offers specify the view rate, thresholds, maximum payout, deadline and content requirements; submissions can be rejected. Its earnings terms prohibit copyright violations in promotional channels. Music claims do not automatically imply a fixed Content Rewards revenue split or payout loss: the actual offer governs. Blocking, rejection or fewer qualifying views can reduce earnings (inference); this Discord campaign's exact music/payout rules were not supplied (unsourced). [Program terms](https://whop.com/content-rewards-terms-of-service/), [earnings terms](https://whop.com/earnings-terms/)
- **Verdict: keep added background music OFF by default.** No direct evidence found that it adds enough benefit over live speech/game audio to offset the clearance/operations burden (unsourced assessment). Use it only when creatively useful and documented for promotional use on every destination, or add separately cleared platform-specific audio. This recommendation follows the platform and campaign restrictions above, not a claim that music always hurts reach. [TikTok](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok), [YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6013276), [Whop](https://whop.com/earnings-terms/)

## A4. Optional FFmpeg layer

Proposed configuration (unsourced design): disabled by default; emit `{asset_id, payoff_seconds, gain}` only after rights/rules review. Convert source-VOD time to edited-clip time; align an impact onset at payoff, or start a riser at `payoff - riser_peak_offset`. Reject events outside the final timeline.

Illustration: input 0 is an already cut 20-second clip; input 1 is a short licensed SFX; onset is 8.5 seconds. Delay/pad the effect, duck **the effect under the original stream**, mix and normalize. This preserves the reaction instead of ducking it for a boom. Filter meanings: [adelay](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#adelay), [sidechaincompress](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaincompress), [amix](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#amix), [loudnorm](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm).

```text
[0:a]aformat=sample_rates=48000:channel_layouts=stereo,asplit=2[main][key];
[1:a]aformat=sample_rates=48000:channel_layouts=stereo,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS,
 volume=0.35,adelay=8500:all=1,apad,atrim=duration=20[fx];
[fx][key]sidechaincompress=threshold=0.03:ratio=6:attack=5:release=120[duck];
[main][duck]amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0,
 loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=7,aresample=48000[outa]
```

Use as `-filter_complex "..." -map 0:v -map "[outa]"`; substitute actual duration/timestamp. For optional music, make another bed and duck it with the same speech key before final mixing. With a composite stream key, loud gameplay also triggers ducking; prefer an isolated mic key when available (design inference). [FFmpeg sidechain semantics](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#sidechaincompress)

Treat approximately -14 LUFS integrated / -1.5 dBTP as an internal starting target, **not** a verified common TikTok/Meta/YouTube delivery requirement (unsourced target). For production use two-pass `loudnorm` with measured I/TP/LRA/threshold/offset, then remeasure the encoded output and audition on phone speakers. The shown single-pass graph passed FFmpeg 9.0.2 with synthetic 20-second main/0.5-second SFX inputs to a null sink; real-clip perceptual quality was not tested. [Normalization options](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#loudnorm)

## B. quso.ai versus Repurpose.io (under 15 lines)

- Volume assumption: 5 finished clips/day cross-posted to one account each on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube = about 150 clips / 450 destination posts per 30 days (task interpretation/arithmetic; unsourced).
- **quso pricing:** Essential **$39/month** includes 300 processing-minute credits and multiplatform scheduling; Growth **$49/month** includes 600 minutes and brand templates. Neither guarantees 150 usable clips: cost depends on input minutes, not output count. At a hypothetical 4-hour VOD/day, input alone is 7,200 minutes/month, beyond both; higher-volume pricing requires contact. Current direct page differs from cached annual offers; use monthly quotes here. [Pricing](https://quso.ai/pricing)
- **quso features/input:** AI clipping, animated captions, reframing, Screen + Speaker layouts and direct Twitch VOD URLs; selection is transcript-based and explicitly weak for silent gameplay. No verified superiority for GTA reactions/hooks/layout quality (unsourced assessment). [Twitch documentation](https://quso.ai/tools/ai-twitch-clip-generator)
- **quso automation:** scheduled/direct posting plus an official MCP connector; its comparison page says no public developer API, so MCP must not be represented as a documented REST API. Exact 450-post/month entitlement/account limits were not conclusively verified; public scheduling text inconsistently discusses 300 posts versus batch size. Treat $39 as the entry tier, not a guaranteed all-in quote. [MCP](https://quso.ai/mcp), [API comparison](https://quso.ai/blog/quso-ai-vs-klap), [scheduler limits](https://quso.ai/products/social-media-scheduler)
- **Repurpose pricing:** Starter **$35/month**, 3 accounts per social network, 5,000 published videos/month: sufficient for the assumed 450 destination posts; annual price $349 prepaid (~$29.08/month). [Pricing](https://repurpose.io/pricing/)
- **Repurpose features/input:** source-to-destination auto-publishing, scheduling, resize/templates and backups; release notes explicitly add Twitch Clips/VODs, with initial Highlights/Clips/Uploads support. Verify the required past-broadcast type in a trial; no proof here of chat-aware automatic highlight discovery. [Features](https://repurpose.io/content-distribution-tool/), [Twitch release notes](https://support.repurpose.io/en/article/release-notes-whats-new-at-repurpose-1y1nxx5/)
- **Repurpose editing/API:** useful for distributing prepared clips; no verified evidence its captions, hooks or facecam layouts beat this pipeline (unsourced). No public API documentation found (unsourced search finding); official partner publishing APIs do not imply customers receive an API. [Publishing mechanism](https://repurpose.io/repurpose-io-vs-alternatives/)
- **Verdict:** retain our pipeline + Upload-Post. Quso offers a hosted review/editor/calendar and MCP convenience; Repurpose offers managed source watching, cross-posting and backups. These are plausible operations advantages, not demonstrated quality/reach wins over our setup; no head-to-head test was run (unsourced assessment). Consider Repurpose only if source watching/distribution maintenance becomes a problem; Quso only for talk-heavy editing/review convenience. [Quso workflow](https://quso.ai/mcp), [Repurpose workflow](https://repurpose.io/content-distribution-tool/)
