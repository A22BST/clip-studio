Labels: **measured** = observational data, **platform** = official documentation, **creator advice** = practitioner judgment, **unsourced** = proposed experiment or evidence gap. ## 1. Hooks in the first 1–2 seconds and retention drop-off

- **Creator advice:** YouTube's interview with Jenny Hoyos advocates hooking within 1 second, then shock/intrigue/satisfaction. This is a successful creator's method, not a measured universal retention threshold. [YouTube, 2025-01-28](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/).
- **Platform, advertising evidence:** TikTok recommends establishing the proposition within 3 seconds and prioritizing the hook within 6 seconds. Its older research says 90% of ad-recall impact occurs within 6 seconds; this does **not** mean 90% of viewers leave at second 6. [TikTok performance-ad guidance, undated, accessed 2026-11-21](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?redirected=1); [Creative Codes, May 2023, underlying recall research 2020](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf).
- **Measured, limited transfer:** OpusClip's 34,635-clip hook sample reports 7-day mean views of 6,037 for product/project showcases (only 40 clips), 5,306 for comparisons (240), and 3,708 for question openers (3,338). These categories do not isolate streamer gaming, spoken versus text hooks, or randomized treatment effects. Do not turn this into a guaranteed hook ranking. [OpusClip original research, 2026-04-03](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-tiktok).
- **Measured retention:** a CHI study of 347 donors and 9.2M TikTok views found 24% skipped before 20% of duration, 40% before halfway, and 45% reached the end. Watch duration was inferred from timestamps; data came from July 2020–February 2022, and 52% of participants were in Africa. These are neither late-2026 gaming benchmarks nor fixed 1–2-second drop-off rates. [Zannettou et al., CHI, 2024-05-11, pp. 5 and 8–9](https://www.franziroesner.com/pdf/zannettou-tiktok-chi24.pdf).
- **Evidence gap:** no directly verified, representative late-2026 second-by-second retention curve for organic streamer clips on all three apps was found (**unsourced: research limitation**). Avoid folklore such as “lose 50% in 2 seconds,” “70% retention guarantees virality,” or a mandatory 200-view test batch (**unsourced: no verified universal thresholds**).

Proposed hook treatments, all **unsourced experiments**, using only events actually present in the source:

| Treatment | First 0–2 seconds | Why test it / failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cold open on the peak | Begin immediately before the visible accident or authentic reaction | Immediate spectacle; may remove the context that makes it funny. |
| Text + original speech | “Kreek trusted this NPC” over the actual exchange | Supplies context silently; do not cover the evidence or invent a betrayal. |
| Spoken hook | Start on his complete, unusual sentence, e.g. an actual “Can I…?” | Preserves his voice; avoid generic greetings and unfinished pronouns. |
| Outcome teaser then setup | Up to 0.5–1 s of reaction, then brief chronological setup | Test only when chronology remains clear; avoid repeating the entire payoff. |
| Specific question | “Can he escape with one tire?” when the clip answers it | Gives a concrete open question; vague “wait for it” is a weaker hypothesis. |

Record retention at 1, 2, 3 and 5 seconds where analytics expose it; compare like-duration clips and keep swipe/hold metrics separate from completion (**unsourced: proposed measurement plan**).

## 2. Clip length and watch-through rate

| Platform | Evidence available | Proposed starting lengths, not proven optima |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok | Buffer's 1.1M-video study: >60 s received 43.2% more median reach and 63.8% more watch time than 30–60 s. Median watch times: 3.1 s for 5–10 s, 6.9 s for 30–60 s, 11.3 s for >60 s. Observational, not gaming-specific. [Buffer, 2025-03-17](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/) | 20–35 s for one joke; 35–60 s for a setup/reaction; test 61–90 s only when the story sustains it (**unsourced**). |
| Instagram Reels | Socialinsider reports median views of 4,700 for 1–30 s, 8,564 for 30–45 s, 10,374 for 45–60 s, and 9,790 for 60–90 s. Its analytics guide attributes length analysis to 6M Reels. These are aggregate views, not completion data. [Length study, updated 2026-10-20](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/); [analytics methodology context, October 2026](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-analytics-guide/) | Test 20–35 s against 45–60 s; use the longer version only when it adds real context (**unsourced**). |
| YouTube Shorts | YouTube permits Shorts up to 3 minutes and ranks using both average duration and average percentage viewed; no official ideal gaming runtime is given. [Creation help, undated](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en); [discovery help, undated](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en) | 15–30 s for a single payoff; 30–60 s for a complete mini-story; do not pad to 60 s (**unsourced**). |

- **Interpretation:** the TikTok study supports testing longer stories; it does not establish that stretching an identical joke improves performance. Its article also inconsistently describes the share of 30–60 s videos, so use the bracket-level performance figures rather than that distribution claim. [Buffer, 2025-03-17](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/).
- Completion and average percentage viewed are different measures: a hypothetical 20 s clip averaging 16 s has 80% average percentage viewed; a 40 s clip averaging 24 s has 60%, yet more seconds watched. Neither tells how many people finished (**unsourced: illustrative arithmetic, not observed results**).
- Do not compare platforms' raw view counts as equal attention. Shorts counts starts/replays without minimum watch time; its engaged-view metric remains separate. [YouTube, change effective 2025-03-31](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en).

## 3. Payoff timing, loops and rewatch

- No verified platform-wide rule says the payoff must land at second 7, 12 or 20; no controlled gaming study establishing an optimal loop seam was found (**unsourced: evidence gap**).
- **Proposed edit map:** hook at 0–1 s; essential context by 3 s; first meaningful action/reaction by 3–5 s; main payoff by 8–15 s in a 15–25 s clip, or 15–30 s in a 30–45 s clip; retain approximately 0.5–2 s of reaction afterward. These are testable editorial defaults, not algorithm facts (**unsourced**).
- If the peak already explains itself, open on it; if the joke requires a rule or expectation, retain that setup. Remove loading, repeated explanations and post-payoff housekeeping, without fabricating a different exchange (**unsourced: recommendation**).
- Test a clean ending that flows into the opening against a normal reaction ending; avoid a black-frame outro or an artificial extra replay. A loop can increase Shorts' public starts/replays, but that alone does not establish more payout-eligible views. [YouTube view definition, effective 2025-03-31](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en); exact edit recommendation **unsourced**.
- Inspect curve spikes as possible replay/attention signals rather than proof a loop caused distribution; a payoff-specific loop lift has not been verified here (**unsourced**).

Using 1080×1920 as the pipeline master is a recommendation, not evidence of a reach multiplier. [TikTok, undated, accessed 2026-11-21](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?redirected=1).

Primary Meta page redirected to login, so these figures are secondary verification and ad guidance, not a certified organic mask. [Anglr, checked 2026-10-12](https://anglr.co/tools/instagram-reels-safe-zone) | Place subtitle blocks above y=1200 and away from action controls; validate in the current organic viewer (**unsourced extrapolation**). |
| Shorts | Google publishes vertical-ad safe-zone templates and says overlays vary by format, screen and campaign. Do not treat an ad template as fixed organic Shorts geometry. [Google Ads specifications, undated](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298?hl=en-0) | Begin with x=120–900, y=300–1200, checking title/channel controls, right button rail and bottom navigation (**unsourced preset**). |

- **Unsourced design defaults:** hook 4–8 words, maximum 2 lines, approximately 72–96 px bold sans-serif; dialogue 52–68 px, 2–5 words per phrase and at most 2 lines. Fit text by rendered width rather than character count. Scale proportionally for other resolutions.
- **Unsourced design defaults:** use a readable bold font such as Inter Bold or Arial Bold; white text with a 4–6 px dark outline or dark backing, one yellow accent for the active word. No verified causal evidence favors yellow, a particular font, ALL CAPS, or a particular pixel size for gaming reach.
- **Unsourced placement defaults:** hook around y=320–500; dialogue near y=850–1100 only when that avoids faces, the crosshair and relevant game UI. Layout-aware placement should override a fixed “captions on the seam” rule.
- **Unsourced test:** phrase-stable captions with one highlighted current word versus individually popping words. Animation should not shift the entire line every word; no verified controlled result establishes word-by-word as universally superior.

## 5. Facecam over gameplay: ratio, crop and zoom

- No credible controlled comparison establishing a winning 30/70, 50/50 or 65/35 gaming split was found; public title/length metadata cannot establish facecam geometry (**unsourced: evidence gap**).
- **Unsourced gameplay-first preset:** top facecam 25–35% of frame height, gameplay 65–75%; begin with 30/70. Crop to include the whole head, chin and enough shoulders to read movement; keep the relevant game action visible rather than blindly center-cropping.
- **Unsourced reaction-first preset:** facecam 55–65%, gameplay 35–45%, used only when the face is the actual payoff. If gameplay is needed to understand the joke, preserve it or briefly cut full-screen to it; do not enlarge a reaction merely because volume peaks.
- **Unsourced crop/zoom defaults:** retain roughly 10–15% headroom; test a brief 1.10–1.20× punch-in at the reaction against no zoom. Upscaling a tiny facecam is not new detail; reject crops with cut-off eyes/mouth or visibly unusable source quality.
- The campaign explicitly requires KreekCraft's overlay and a return path to his stream. Treat these as constraints on every crop, including zoomed frames. [Campaign, undated, accessed 2026-11-21](https://contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/2eaa14fb-6927-4136-897e-b096e0d907d6).

## 6. Subtitle practice

- **Accessibility, not virality proof:** W3C calls for synchronized captions including dialogue, speaker identification and meaningful non-speech sounds; captions should not obstruct relevant visuals. [W3C WCAG caption guidance, undated, accessed 2026-11-21](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded).
- TikTok supports automatically generated captions and creator corrections; that establishes a correction workflow, not a measured reach uplift. [TikTok, 2021-04-06](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-auto-captions?lang=en).
- OpusClip lists captions in 80.2% and animation in 78.6% of its editing-feature sample. Its page alternates between “all clips” and “viral clips,” and the displayed counts do not neatly reconcile with the headline 13.5M denominator. Treat this as vendor prevalence evidence, not proof captions cause virality. [OpusClip, 2026-04-03](https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-tiktok).
- **Unsourced production defaults:** caption all intelligible relevant speech; manually check KreekCraft, GTA names, slang, overlapping game dialogue and screams. Use speaker labels only when needed; mark a meaningful sound such as “[crash]” when it explains the reaction.
- **Unsourced production defaults:** align words to audible speech within a proposed ±100 ms tolerance; break at natural phrases, avoid displaying the punchline before it is said, and test readability on a phone with sound off. Keep the hook and dialogue visually distinct.
- **Unsourced production defaults:** retain a corrected transcript alongside the burned-caption master; provide native/closed captions where supported and check for overlapping duplicates. Match any campaign-required censoring in audio and text without changing the meaning.

## Ranked top 10 changes for an automated clip pipeline

Ranking reflects this campaign's practical priorities; it is **unsourced editorial judgment**, not a measured order of uplift.

1. **Validate campaign eligibility before rendering/posting:** version rate/minimum/cap, 40% country gate, required logo, stream attribution and tags; reconcile the conflicting public rates. [Campaign, accessed 2026-11-21](https://contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/2eaa14fb-6927-4136-897e-b096e0d907d6); [older brief](https://beta.contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/2eaa14fb-6927-4136-897e-b096e0d907d6).
2. **Select a complete event, not merely a loud segment:** require identifiable setup, action and authentic reaction; score whether a stranger understands the first second. Exact scoring is **unsourced**; rationale: [YouTube creator interview, 2025-01-28](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/).
3. **Generate candidate 0–2 s openings for human selection:** original spoken opening, truthful text context, or peak-first cold open; record treatment without posting near-duplicates as an experiment (**unsourced proposed workflow**).
4. **Trim around the payoff:** remove dead lead-in and trailing housekeeping; use section 3's timing as test defaults, never a hard rejection rule (**unsourced**).
5. **Render adaptive camera/game layouts:** test 30/70 gameplay-first and 60/40 reaction-first, preserve the overlay and game evidence, and reject failed crops (**unsourced geometry**). [Campaign overlay rule, undated](https://contentrewards.com/c/campaigns/2eaa14fb-6927-4136-897e-b096e0d907d6).
6. **Add platform UI preview masks:** verify hook, captions, disclosure and logo in the actual app; distinguish ad templates from organic geometry. [TikTok specs, undated](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-reservation-in-feed-ads-reach-frequency?lang=el-GR); [Google specs, undated](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298?hl=en-0).
7. **Correct and synchronize subtitles:** names, speaker changes, meaningful game sounds and punchline timing before decorative animation. [W3C caption guidance, undated](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded); exact tolerance/style **unsourced**.
8. **Keep duration flexible by story/platform:** compare short and longer complete edits; do not mandate 60 s solely for a separate native rewards program. [Buffer, 2025-03-17](https://buffer.com/resources/longer-tiktoks-get-more-views-data/); [TikTok Creator Rewards, 2024-03-18](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-the-new-creator-rewards-program/?lang=en).
9. **Schedule regional experiments with submission deadlines:** use section 7's starting slots and DST-aware clocks; track the post URL promptly so the campaign's 30-minute submission window is met. [Content Rewards terms, 2026-11-17](https://contentrewards.com/terms); regional schedule **unsourced hypothesis**.
10. **Optimize eligible earnings with retained analytics:** log public/engaged/verified views separately, geography, approval and fees; rank experiments by qualifying clips and net payout alongside retention, not total public views (**unsourced implementation recommendation**). [YouTube view definitions, effective 2025-03-31](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en); [Content Rewards verification/fees, 2026-11-17](https://contentrewards.com/terms).
